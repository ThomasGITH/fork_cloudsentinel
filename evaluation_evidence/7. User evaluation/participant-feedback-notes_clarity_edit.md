# Anonymised participant notes

These notes are a collection of all the observation recorded during the three expert walkthroughs.The participants are called P1, P2 and P3 for anonimity sake. (note that this is not the actual notes made during the session, just a cleanup/edited version of it).

## P1

- Live Monitoring was viewed positively and considered visually clear.
- The discussion focused mainly on the Model Comparisons screen.
- The application appeared more suitable for researchers than for operational teams.
- The individual components became clear after explanation, but it was not immediately clear where a new user should begin or how the complete workflow should be followed.

## P2

- The visual interface for adding detectors was considered useful, especially for users with less experience creating detector configurations manually.
- The participant did not immediately identify a personal use case for the manual dataset and training process. A more ready-to-use form of anomaly detection was preferred for operational use.
- The meaning of F1 was not immediately clear. Terms in the Robustness view, including precision categories and runtime, required more explanation.
- Without guidance, it would be difficult to know where to begin.
- Operational monitoring was described as an ongoing process. Patterns that occur regularly may later be accepted as normal, leading operators to adjust thresholds or monitoring rules.
- Suggested improvements included allowing operator feedback, marking repeated patterns as normal, and periodically updating models using recent monitoring data.
- Live Monitoring was considered the most practically useful part. Showing an anomaly together with a probable cause was seen as valuable, although the next investigation step would still use cluster information, logs, traces, and dependency information.
- The dataset and training process was considered too manual for routine operational use.

## P3

- The amount of manual input was considered an important limitation for managed-services use.
- Training parameters required clearer names and explanations.
- The Robustness view would benefit from information buttons or explanatory columns.
- Many individual screens were understandable after their purpose had been explained.
- Live Monitoring was viewed positively because it showed a probable cause alongside the detected anomaly.
- The overall structure was less clear than the individual components. It was not immediately clear where to start, where to configure the Prometheus connection, or how the separate setup steps formed one workflow.
